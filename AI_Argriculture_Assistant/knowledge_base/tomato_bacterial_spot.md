# Tomato Bacterial Spot Management

- **Title**: Diagnosis and Management of Bacterial Spot in Tomato
- **Source**: ICAR - Indian Institute of Vegetable Research (IIVR), Varanasi
- **Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)
- **Disease**: Bacterial Spot (*Xanthomonas perforans* / *Xanthomonas campestris pv. vesicatoria*)
- **Reference**: IIVR Technical Bulletin: Integrated Disease Management in Tomato

## Symptoms
Small, circular to angular, dark brown to black water-soaked lesions appear on leaves, stems, and pedicels. Lesions often become surrounded by a narrow yellow chlorotic halo. Under high disease pressure, spots coalesce causing leaf blighting, yellowing, and severe defoliation that exposes developing fruit to sunscald. On green fruits, small, slightly raised black blisters emerge which later become scab-like with rough brown centers.

## Favorable Environmental Conditions
High temperatures (24°C to 30°C) with prolonged relative humidity (>85%) and wind-driven rain or overhead irrigation. The bacterium enters leaves through natural openings (stomata, hydathodes) and microscopic wounds.

## Cultural & Preventative Management
1. Use certified disease-free seeds. Treat seeds with hot water at 50°C for 25 minutes or soak in 0.5% sodium hypochlorite for 1 minute before sowing.
2. Space plants adequately (75 cm x 60 cm) and stake tomato vines to improve canopy ventilation and accelerate leaf drying.
3. Strict drip irrigation practice to prevent water splash dissemination.
4. Rotate with non-solanaceous crops such as cereals, legumes, or brassicas for 2–3 seasons.
5. Apply *Pseudomonas fluorescens* (5 g/L) or *Bacillus subtilis* foliar sprays at transplanting and early vegetative stage.

## Recommended Chemical Control
1. Spray Copper Oxychloride 50% WP @ 2.5–3.0 g/L or Copper Hydroxide 77% WP @ 2.0 g/L.
2. Tank mix Copper Oxychloride with Streptocycline (Streptomycin sulphate + Tetracycline hydrochloride) @ 100 ppm (1 g per 10 L water).
3. Re-spray at 10-day intervals if warm, cloudy, and wet weather persists.

## Withholding Period & Safety
Avoid spraying during active pollination hours. Wear protective equipment and respect a 5-day pre-harvest interval after copper application.
