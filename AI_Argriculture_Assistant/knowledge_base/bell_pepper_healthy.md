# Good Agricultural Practices for Healthy Bell Pepper

- **Title**: Integrated Crop Management for Healthy Bell Pepper / Capsicum Production
- **Source**: ICAR - National Research Centre for Integrated Pest Management (NCIPM), New Delhi
- **Crop**: Bell Pepper (Capsicum annuum)
- **Topic**: Healthy Foliage, Nutrient Balance, and Preventative IPM
- **Reference**: ICAR IPM Guidelines for Horticultural Crops

## Characteristics of Healthy Foliage
Healthy bell pepper leaves are uniformly deep green, turgid, and free of chlorotic speckles, necrotic spots, or upward cupping. Stems are sturdy and erect with active vegetative apex growth.

## Soil & Nutrient Management
1. Well-drained sandy loam or clay loam soil with pH 6.0 to 7.0 is ideal.
2. Incorporate well-decomposed Farmyard Manure (FYM) or compost @ 20–25 tonnes/ha during final land preparation.
3. Apply balanced N:P:K fertilizers based on soil test recommendations (typically 120:60:60 kg/ha in split doses).
4. Micronutrient foliar application: Spray 0.2% Zinc sulphate and 0.1% Borax at flower initiation stage to avoid blossom-end rot and leaf distortion.

## Water & Canopy Management
1. Regulate moisture evenly using drip lines; avoid water-logging at all costs.
2. Maintain adequate spacing (60 cm x 45 cm) to promote airflow and minimize microclimatic humidity in the inner canopy.
3. Mulch with silver-black polyethylene (25–30 micron) or organic straw to suppress weed competition and soil splashing.

## Preventative Pest & Disease Monitoring
1. Install yellow and blue sticky traps (10–12 traps/acre) to monitor whiteflies, thrips, and aphids early.
2. Spray 0.5% Neem oil (5 ml/L) as a broad-spectrum organic preventive spray every 15 days.
3. Regularly inspect lower leaf surfaces for initial signs of pests or lesions.
