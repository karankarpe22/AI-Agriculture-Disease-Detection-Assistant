# Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV) Management

- **Title**: Integrated Vector and Disease Management for Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus
- **Source**: ICAR - Indian Institute of Horticultural Research (IIHR), Bengaluru
- **Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)
- **Disease**: Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV, *Begomovirus*)
- **Vector**: Whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*)
- **Reference**: IIHR Farmer Advisory on Tomato Virus Management

## Symptoms
Infected tomato plants show severe stunting, erect bushy growth, and shortened internodes. Leaves become greatly reduced in size, curl upward and inward (cupping), become puckered and crinkled, and show pronounced interveinal chlorosis (yellowing) with pale green margins. Plants infected early in the season remain severely dwarfed and flower blossoms drop off without setting fruit, leading to total yield loss.

## Transmission & Epidemiology
TYLCV is not transmitted through seed or mechanical touch; it is persistently transmitted exclusively by the whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*). Warm, dry weather encourages high whitefly populations and rapid virus dissemination across fields.

## Integrated Vector & Disease Management
1. **Nursery Protection**:
   - Raise tomato nursery under insect-proof net house (40–50 mesh nylon net) to prevent whiteflies from feeding on seedlings before transplanting.
   - Drench seedlings with Thiamethoxam 25% WG @ 0.3 g/L or Imidacloprid 17.8% SL @ 0.5 ml/L 5 days prior to field transplanting.
2. **Field Management**:
   - Use virus-tolerant/resistant commercial hybrids (e.g., Arka Rakshak, Arka Samrat, US-440).
   - Install yellow sticky traps @ 20–25 traps/acre evenly at canopy height to capture adult whiteflies.
   - Grow 2–3 border rows of tall barrier crops (maize, pearl millet, or sorghum) 20 days prior to transplanting to obstruct whitefly flight.
   - Mulch with reflective silver-colored polyethylene film to repel incoming whiteflies.
3. **Chemical Vector Control (Early Field Stage)**:
   - Diafenthiuron 50% WP @ 1.2 g/L, OR
   - Cyantraniliprole 10.26% OD @ 1.8 ml/L, OR
   - Spiromesifen 22.9% SC @ 1.0 ml/L, OR
   - Acetamiprid 20% SP @ 0.3 g/L.
4. Rogue and burn or bury infected plants immediately upon early symptom detection to reduce local virus reservoir.

## Precautions
Insecticides kill the insect vector, not the virus inside the plant. Once a plant is systemically infected with TYLCV, it cannot be cured; prevention and early vector control are paramount.
