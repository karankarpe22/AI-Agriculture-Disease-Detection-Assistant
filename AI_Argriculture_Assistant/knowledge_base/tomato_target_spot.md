# Tomato Target Spot Management

- **Title**: Diagnosis and Management of Target Spot in Tomato
- **Source**: ICAR - Indian Institute of Vegetable Research (IIVR), Varanasi
- **Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)
- **Disease**: Target Spot (*Corynespora cassiicola*)
- **Reference**: ICAR Crop Protection Guide for Solanaceous Crops

## Symptoms
Target spot produces small, brown, pinpoint spots on foliage that enlarge into circular to irregular lesions with pale brown to tan centers and distinct concentric brown rings (hence "target spot"). Lesions are often surrounded by a prominent yellow chlorotic halo. Severe infections cause extensive premature defoliation starting from the lower canopy upwards. On green fruit, small, slightly sunken circular lesions with light brown centers develop that cause cracking and soft rot.

## Favorable Environmental Conditions
Warm temperatures (25°C to 32°C) combined with high relative humidity (>80%) and prolonged leaf wetness (longer than 12 hours). Dense canopies with poor ventilation exacerbate disease incidence.

## Cultural & Preventative Practices
1. Avoid overhead irrigation and ensure foliage dries quickly after rain.
2. Space rows at 90 cm x 60 cm to allow wind penetration throughout the canopy.
3. Remove old, senescent lower leaves to reduce proximity to soil moisture.
4. Rotate with non-host monocot crops (sorghum, corn, pearl millet) for 2 seasons.

## Recommended Fungicides
1. Chlorothalonil 75% WP @ 2.0 g/L as a protective preventative spray.
2. Azoxystrobin 18.2% + Difenoconazole 11.4% SC @ 1.0 ml/L.
3. Fluxapyroxad + Pyraclostrobin @ 0.6 ml/L.
4. Mancozeb 75% WP @ 2.5 g/L applied every 10 days during cloudy, humid weather.

## Precautions
Rotate fungicide classes to prevent resistance. Maintain recommended dilution rates to avoid leaf tip burn.
