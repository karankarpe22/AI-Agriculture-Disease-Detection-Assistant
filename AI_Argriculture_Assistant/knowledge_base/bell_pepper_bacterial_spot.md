# Bacterial Spot of Bell Pepper (Capsicum)

- **Title**: Management of Bacterial Spot in Bell Pepper / Capsicum
- **Source**: ICAR - Indian Institute of Horticultural Research (IIHR), Bengaluru & TNAU Agritech Portal
- **Crop**: Bell Pepper (Capsicum annuum)
- **Disease**: Bacterial Spot (*Xanthomonas euvesicatoria* / *Xanthomonas campestris pv. vesicatoria*)
- **Reference**: ICAR Package of Practices for Vegetable Crops; TNAU Plant Pathology Crop Protection Compendium

## Symptoms
Small, water-soaked, circular to irregular lesions appear primarily on the underside of leaves. As the lesions expand, they turn dark brown to black with a greasy or water-soaked border and may develop yellow halos. Leaf spots lead to severe chlorosis and extensive premature defoliation. On fruit, raised blister-like or scabby brown spots occur.

## Favorable Environmental Conditions
Warm temperatures (25°C to 30°C) combined with high relative humidity (>85%) and frequent rainfall, overhead irrigation, or heavy dew favor rapid pathogen spread. Splashing water facilitates leaf-to-leaf dissemination.

## Cultural & Biological Management
1. Use certified disease-free seeds and disease-free nursery seedlings.
2. Hot water seed treatment at 50°C for 25 minutes to eliminate seed-borne inoculum.
3. Adopt drip irrigation rather than overhead sprinklers to keep foliage dry.
4. Practice 2 to 3 years crop rotation with non-solanaceous crops (e.g., maize, pulses).
5. Remove and safely destroy all crop residues after harvest.
6. Spray biological antagonist *Pseudomonas fluorescens* (2 g/L) or *Bacillus subtilis* (2 g/L) at nursery stage and early vegetative growth.

## Recommended Chemical Management
1. Copper hydroxide 77% WP @ 2.0 g/L or Copper oxychloride 50% WP @ 2.5–3.0 g/L.
2. Streptocycline (Streptomycin sulphate + Tetracycline hydrochloride) @ 100 ppm (1 g per 10 litres of water) tank-mixed with copper oxychloride at early appearance of symptoms.
3. Repeat spray at 10 to 14 day intervals depending on weather and disease pressure.

## Precautions & Withholding Period
Avoid excessive copper accumulation in sandy soils. Do not spray during peak noon heat to prevent phytotoxicity. Observe a minimum withholding period of 3 days before harvesting fruits.
