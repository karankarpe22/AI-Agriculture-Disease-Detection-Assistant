# Two-Spotted Spider Mite Management in Tomato

- **Title**: Integrated Pest Management for Two-Spotted Spider Mite in Tomato
- **Source**: ICAR - National Bureau of Agricultural Insect Resources (NBAIR) & DPPQS
- **Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)
- **Pest/Damage**: Two-spotted Spider Mite (*Tetranychus urticae*)
- **Reference**: ICAR-NBAIR Pest Management Advisory for Horticultural Crops

## Symptoms & Identification
Spider mites live on the underside of tomato leaves where they puncture plant cells with their stylets and suck sap. The upper leaf surface displays characteristic fine yellow or white stippling (speckling). As mite populations surge, leaves turn bronzed, leathery, or desiccated, and extensive fine webbing covers leaf surfaces, shoot terminals, and flower clusters. Premature leaf drop and stunted growth result under heavy infestation.

## Favorable Environmental Conditions
Hot, dry, and dusty weather (temperatures >30°C and relative humidity <50%) accelerates spider mite reproduction; a single generation can complete in just 7 to 10 days. Water stress severely exacerbates mite damage.

## Cultural & Biological Control
1. Maintain optimum soil moisture; avoid water stress during hot dry spells.
2. Overhead sprinkler or water jet spraying can dislodge mites and disrupt web-building.
3. Conserve and augment natural predatory mites (e.g., *Neoseiulus californicus*, *Phytoseiulus persimilis*).
4. Spray botanical formulations: Neem oil (Azadirachtin 10,000 ppm) @ 2.0–3.0 ml/L or Pongamia oil @ 5.0 ml/L.
5. Destroy broadleaf weed hosts (*Amaranthus*, *Chenopodium*) around field boundaries.

## Recommended Acaricides (Chemical Control)
1. Spiromesifen 22.9% SC @ 1.0 ml/L (highly effective against nymph and egg stages).
2. Propargite 57% EC @ 2.0 ml/L (effective contact adulticide).
3. Fenazaquin 10% EC @ 1.5–2.0 ml/L, OR
4. Abamectin 1.9% EC @ 0.75–1.0 ml/L.
5. Ensure high-pressure spray directed specifically at the undersides of the foliage.

## Safety & Waiting Period
Do not use broad-spectrum synthetic pyrethroids (e.g., Cypermethrin) as they eliminate natural predatory mites and cause catastrophic mite flare-ups. Observe a minimum 5-day withholding period before harvest.
