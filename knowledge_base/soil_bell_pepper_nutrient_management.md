# Bell Pepper Soil Requirements, Salinity Sensitivity and Drainage

- **Title**: Bell Pepper (Capsicum) Soil Health, Aeration and Root Nutrition
- **Source**: ICAR - Indian Institute of Vegetable Research (IIVR), Varanasi & TNAU
- **Crop**: Bell Pepper / Capsicum (*Capsicum annuum*)
- **Topic**: Capsicum Soil Aeration, Salinity Tolerance and Micronutrients
- **Reference**: TNAU Agritech Portal & Commercial Capsicum Production Manual

## Soil Aeration, Drainage and Texture
Bell pepper (Capsicum) has a fibrous and relatively shallow root system (70% of active feeding roots are in the upper 20-30 cm soil layer).
- Optimal Soil Type: Highly well-drained sandy loam, rich loam, or light clay loam with high organic matter (>1.0%).
- Intolerance to Waterlogging: Capsicum is one of the most sensitive horticultural crops to poor soil aeration. Anaerobic soil conditions for even 12 hours cause leaf epinasty (downward leaf curling), flower drop, and rapid outbreak of Phytophthora root and crown rot (*Phytophthora capsici*).
- Raised Bed & Mulching: Planting on 20 cm high raised beds with silver-black polyethylene mulch regulates root-zone soil temperature, prevents weed competition, and maintains uniform soil moisture.

## Salinity Sensitivity and pH Requirements
- Ideal pH Range: 6.0 to 6.8.
- Salinity Hazard: Bell pepper is classified as moderately sensitive to soil salinity. Electrical Conductivity (EC) of saturation extract exceeding 1.5 dS/m leads to yield reduction of up to 25%, stunted canopy growth, and blossom end rot. In saline soils or with brackish irrigation water, frequent light irrigations and leaching fractions are necessary.

## Organic Manuring and Micronutrient Balance
- Soil-borne Disease Suppression: Incorporation of 250-500 kg/ha of neem cake into planting beds effectively suppresses plant-parasitic nematodes (*Meloidogyne incognita*) and provides slow-release nitrogen.
- Micronutrient Balance: In high pH or calcareous soils, bell pepper frequently exhibits zinc deficiency (little leaf, rosetting) and iron chlorosis (interveinal yellowing of youngest leaves). Basal soil application of zinc sulphate (20-25 kg/ha) or foliar application of chelated zinc (1.0 g/L) and ferrous sulphate (2.0 g/L with citric acid) prevents physiological deficiencies.
