# Tomato Soil Health, Nutrition and Rhizosphere Management

- **Title**: Tomato Soil Requirements, Nutrient Dynamics and Root-Zone Management
- **Source**: ICAR - Indian Institute of Horticultural Research (IIHR), Bengaluru & TNAU Agritech Portal
- **Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)
- **Topic**: Tomato Soil Requirements, Salinity and Calcium Nutrition
- **Reference**: ICAR Package of Practices for Tomato Production & Plant Nutrition

## Optimal Soil Type and Reaction
Tomato thrives in deep, fertile, well-drained loams, sandy loams, or medium black soils rich in organic matter.
- Ideal pH range: 6.0 to 7.0. Tomatoes tolerate mild acidity down to pH 5.5 and mild alkalinity up to pH 7.8.
- Soil Drainage: Tomato roots are extremely sensitive to poor drainage and waterlogging. Subsurface saturation (>24 hours) causes rapid root asphyxiation, wilting, and triggers soil-borne fungal pathogens (*Fusarium oxysporum*, *Pythium*, and *Phytophthora*).
- Raised bed cultivation (15-20 cm height, 90-120 cm width) with drip lateral lines is strongly recommended on heavy black soils (Vertisols) to maintain optimal soil aeration and prevent root diseases.

## Blossom End Rot and Calcium Moisture Interaction
Blossom End Rot (BER) is a physiological disorder characterized by water-soaked, sunken, leathery black spots at the blossom end of green or ripening tomato fruits.
- Primary Cause: Calcium deficiency in rapidly expanding fruit tissue. This is almost never caused by a simple lack of soil calcium, but rather by fluctuating soil moisture (alternating cycles of drought stress and excessive irrigation) or high soil salinity/EC, which impairs calcium transpirational pull through xylem vessels.
- Management: Maintain consistent root-zone moisture (soil moisture tension 20-30 kPa). Apply agricultural gypsum (calcium sulphate) at 250-500 kg/ha in calcareous or calcium-depleted soils. Apply foliar sprays of calcium nitrate (0.5%) or chelated calcium (0.2%) at flowering and early fruit development stages.

## Balanced Fertigation and Nutrient Partitioning
Nutrient requirements of tomato vary across physiological stages:
1. Basal Stage: 25-30% of total N, 100% of P (as single superphosphate SSP or DAP), and 25% of K incorporated with organic manure. High phosphorus is critical for early root development.
2. Vegetative to Flowering Stage: Balanced N and K application. Avoid excess nitrogen which promotes excessive succulent foliage, delayed flowering, and vulnerability to suckling pests (whiteflies, aphids) and Early Blight.
3. Fruit Bulking and Harvesting Stage: Heavy potassium demand. Potassium promotes fruit firmness, uniform red pigmentation (lycopene synthesis), total soluble solids (Brix), and shelf life. Apply water-soluble potassium nitrate (13-0-45) or sulphate of potash (0-0-50) via drip fertigation.
