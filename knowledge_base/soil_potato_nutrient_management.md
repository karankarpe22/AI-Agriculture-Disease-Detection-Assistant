# Potato Soil Management, Acidic Preference and Tuberization

- **Title**: Potato Soil Health, Soil Compaction and Tuberization Dynamics
- **Source**: ICAR - Central Potato Research Institute (CPRI), Shimla
- **Crop**: Potato (*Solanum tuberosum*)
- **Topic**: Potato Soil Requirements, Compaction and Common Scab Prevention
- **Reference**: CPRI Agronomic Guidelines for Commercial Potato Cultivation

## Optimal Soil Physical Conditions and pH Range
Potato is a subterranean tuber crop requiring a loose, friable, well-aerated sandy loam, silt loam, or light alluvial soil with high organic matter content.
- Ideal pH Range: 5.2 to 6.4 (moderately acidic to slightly acidic).
- Common Scab (*Streptomyces scabies*) Warning: Alkaline or limed soils (pH > 6.8 - 7.5) significantly increase the incidence of Common Scab, which causes rough corky lesions on tuber skins. In alkaline soils, avoid liming and use physiologically acidic fertilizers such as ammonium sulphate or apply elemental sulphur.
- Bulk Density and Compaction: Potato roots and stolons cannot penetrate dense or compacted soil layers. Bulk density should remain below 1.35 g/cm³. Hardpan formation causes distorted, irregular, knobby tubers and drastically reduces commercial marketable grade. Deep summer subsoiling followed by fine tilth preparation is essential.

## Tuber Bulking and Soil Moisture Regulation
Soil moisture within the 0 to 30 cm tuber zone governs tuber initiation and expansion:
- Optimal moisture regime: 65% to 75% of field capacity throughout tuber initiation (20-35 days after planting) and tuber bulking (35-70 days after planting).
- Uneven irrigation (drought followed by heavy irrigation) causes growth cracks, hollow heart, and secondary knobby tuber growth.
- Stagnant water or saturated soil during tuber development restricts gas exchange, promotes lenticel proliferation, and causes soft rot (*Pectobacterium carotovorum*) in the field and storage.

## Fertilizer Dynamics and Potassium Nutrition
Potato is an exhaustive, heavy consumer of soil nutrients:
- Potassium: Highest nutrient uptake. Potassium Sulphate (SOP - 0-0-50) is strongly preferred over Muriate of Potash (MOP - 0-0-60) because chloride ions reduce tuber starch content, specific gravity, and processing chip quality.
- Organic Matter: Responds dramatically to green manuring or 25 tonnes/ha of well-rotted FYM. Bio-fertilizer treatment of seed tubers with Azotobacter and PSB enhances nutrient uptake efficiency.
