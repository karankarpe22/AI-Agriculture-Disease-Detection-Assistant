## Soil Orders and Regional Characteristics
Indian agricultural soils are primarily classified into major orders:
1. Vertisols and Vertic Inceptisols (Black Cotton Soils): Prevalent across Maharashtra (Deccan basaltic plateau), Madhya Pradesh, and Gujarat. High clay content (>40%, predominantly smectite/montmorillonite), high swelling and shrinking characteristics, deep crack formation during dry periods, high moisture holding capacity, and moderate to high cation exchange capacity (CEC 35-55 cmol(c)/kg). Typical pH is neutral to moderately alkaline (7.5 - 8.4).
2. Inceptisols and Entisols (Alluvial and Medium Black Soils): Widespread in river valleys and transitional plains. Variable texture from sandy loam to clay loam, good drainage, moderate fertility, and pH ranging from 6.5 to 7.8.
3. Alfisols and Ultisols (Red and Lateritic Soils): Common in Western Ghats, Konkan coastal strip, and plateau fringes. Coarse to medium textured, prone to acidity (pH 5.0 - 6.5), low water holding capacity, and high phosphorus fixation due to iron and aluminum oxides.

## Soil Reaction pH and Correction Protocols
Soil pH controls the chemical bioavailability of essential plant macro and micro-nutrients:
- Strongly Acidic (pH < 5.5): Severe phosphorus fixation, aluminum/manganese toxicity, and reduced calcium/magnesium availability. Remediation requires agricultural lime (calcium carbonate CaCO3) or dolomite application at 1.5 to 3.0 tonnes/ha based on soil buffer capacity, incorporated 3-4 weeks prior to transplanting.
- Optimal Neutral (pH 6.2 - 7.2): Maximum nutrient availability for microbial nitrogen fixation, soluble phosphorus, and balanced cation uptake.
- Moderately Alkaline / Calcareous (pH 7.8 - 8.5): Common in Deccan Vertisols. High free calcium carbonate causes fixation of phosphorus (into insoluble calcium phosphates) and micronutrient deficiencies (iron chlorosis, zinc deficiency). Remediation includes regular addition of organic amendments (FYM, compost), sulphur application (150-250 kg/ha), gypsum for sodic conditions, and foliar feeding of chelated micronutrients (Fe-EDTA, Zn-EDTA).

## Soil Organic Carbon and Biological Fertility
Soil Organic Carbon (SOC) is the primary driver of soil biological activity, aggregate stability, and moisture retention:
- Low SOC (<0.50%): Common in semi-arid tropical soils due to rapid microbial oxidation under high temperatures. Indicates poor microbial biodiversity and low nutrient buffer capacity.
- Medium SOC (0.50% - 0.75%): Moderate fertility requiring maintenance.
- High SOC (>0.75%): Desirable biological fertility.
Recommended Management Practices:
1. Basal incorporation of well-decomposed Farmyard Manure (FYM) or compost at 10-15 tonnes/ha or vermicompost at 2.5-5 tonnes/ha before sowing.
2. In-situ green manuring with Sunnhemp (*Crotalaria juncea*) or Dhaincha (*Sesbania aculeata*) incorporated at 45-50 days (flowering stage), adding 15-20 tonnes/ha of biomass and 60-80 kg N/ha.
3. Inoculation with bio-fertilizers: Azotobacter / Azospirillum for non-symbiotic nitrogen fixation, and Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria (PSB - *Bacillus megaterium* or *Pseudomonas striata*) at 5 kg/ha mixed with FYM.

## Macronutrient Indices and Laboratory Soil Testing
Official Indian Soil Health Card critical nutrient limits:
- Available Nitrogen (Alkaline Permanganate method): Low (<280 kg/ha), Medium (280 - 560 kg/ha), High (>560 kg/ha).
- Available Phosphorus (Olsen method for neutral/alkaline soils): Low (<10 kg P/ha or <23 kg P2O5/ha), Medium (10 - 25 kg P/ha), High (>25 kg P/ha).
- Available Potassium (Neutral Ammonium Acetate method): Low (<110 kg K/ha or <133 kg K2O/ha), Medium (110 - 280 kg K/ha), High (>280 kg K2O/ha).
- Salinity Hazard (Electrical Conductivity 1:2 soil-water extract): Non-saline (<1.0 dS/m), Slightly saline / sensitive crop injury (1.0 - 2.0 dS/m), Severely saline (>4.0 dS/m).
Farmers must have a representative composite field soil sample tested at an authorized Soil Testing Laboratory (STL) or Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) every 2-3 years to establish exact field fertilizer schedules.
