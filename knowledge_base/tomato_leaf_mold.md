# Tomato Leaf Mold Management

- **Title**: Diagnosis and Control of Leaf Mold in Tomato
- **Source**: Tamil Nadu Agricultural University (TNAU) Agritech Portal & ICAR
- **Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)
- **Disease**: Leaf Mold (*Passalora fulva*, syn. *Cladosporium fulvum*)
- **Reference**: TNAU Crop Protection Compendium for Horticultural Crops

## Symptoms
Initial symptoms manifest as pale yellow to light green diffuse patches on the upper surface of older leaves. Directly beneath these yellow patches on the lower leaf surface, an olive-green, tan to velvety purplish-brown fungal mold forms. As the fungus sporulates profusely, the yellow spots turn brown, and the entire leaf curls, withers, and prematurely drops off. Blossoms and young fruit stems may abort under severe infections.

## Favorable Environmental Conditions
Warm temperatures (21°C to 27°C) accompanied by very high relative humidity (>85%). Particularly destructive in protected cultivation (polyhouses, shade net structures) and densely planted, poorly ventilated field stands.

## Cultural & Environmental Management
1. Enhance ventilation: Open side curtains in greenhouses and increase row spacing in open fields to decrease leaf wetness duration.
2. Maintain relative humidity below 80% through proper ventilation and avoid overhead misting.
3. Prune suckers and lower senescent leaves to allow sunlight and wind penetration.
4. Hot water seed treatment at 50°C for 25 minutes to eradicate seed-borne fungal spores.

## Recommended Fungicidal Control
1. Copper Oxychloride 50% WP @ 2.5 g/L or Bordeaux Mixture 1.0%.
2. Chlorothalonil 75% WP @ 2.0 g/L as a protective preventative spray.
3. Difenoconazole 25% EC @ 1.0 ml/L or Azoxystrobin 23% SC @ 1.0 ml/L at first sign of velvety sporulation on the underside of leaves.
4. Ensure spray droplets thoroughly wet the underside of the foliage where fungal sporulation occurs.

## Precautions
Do not apply copper sprays during very cloudy, damp weather when spray dry-time exceeds 3 hours, as leaf burn may occur.
