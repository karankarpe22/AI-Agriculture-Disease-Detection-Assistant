# Tomato Septoria Leaf Spot Management

- **Title**: Diagnosis and Integrated Management of Septoria Leaf Spot in Tomato
- **Source**: ICAR - Indian Institute of Vegetable Research (IIVR) & State Agricultural Universities
- **Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)
- **Disease**: Septoria Leaf Spot (*Septoria lycopersici*)
- **Reference**: ICAR Vegetable Pathology Compendium

## Symptoms
Numerous tiny, circular spots (1.5 to 3 mm in diameter) appear on older lower leaves. The spots characteristically have dark brown to black borders with pale white or ash-grey centers. Within mature spots, tiny black specks (pycnidia / fungal fruiting bodies) are clearly visible under magnification. Severely affected leaves turn completely yellow, wither, and drop, creating severe upward defoliation that exposes fruits to solar injury and reduces yields.

## Favorable Environmental Conditions
Warm temperatures (20°C to 26°C) combined with high relative humidity and prolonged leaf wetness (longer than 8 hours). Rain splashes or overhead irrigation easily detach and splash spores from infected lower debris onto upper leaves.

## Cultural & Preventative Practices
1. Crop rotation: Rotate fields away from tomato, potato, or eggplant for 2–3 seasons.
2. Clean cultivation: Remove and destroy tomato plant residue and solanaceous weeds immediately after final harvest.
3. Mulch and stake: Lay plastic or straw mulch and stake plants upright to eliminate contact between bottom leaves and wet soil.
4. Bottom leaf pruning: Strip lower leaves up to 25 cm from the soil line once crown clusters have set.

## Recommended Fungicidal Control
1. **Preventive Foliar Sprays**:
   - Chlorothalonil 75% WP @ 2.0 g/L, OR
   - Mancozeb 75% WP @ 2.5 g/L, OR
   - Copper Oxychloride 50% WP @ 2.5 g/L.
2. **Systemic Curative Sprays (At disease onset)**:
   - Azoxystrobin 18.2% + Difenoconazole 11.4% SC @ 1.0 ml/L, OR
   - Pyraclostrobin 20% WG @ 1.0 g/L, OR
   - Tebuconazole 25.9% EC @ 1.0 ml/L.
3. Reapply at 10 to 14 day intervals depending on rain frequency.

## Precautions
Avoid walking through wet fields during morning dew or after rain to avoid spreading pycnidiospores on clothing and tools.
